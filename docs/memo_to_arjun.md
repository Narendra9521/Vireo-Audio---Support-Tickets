**To:** Arjun Mehta, Finance Controller  **Re:** Refund summary for the board pack (Jan 2025 - Jun 2026)

**Bottom line.** Refunds are about **Rs 67 lakh over 18 months, Rs 12-16 lakh a quarter**, not Rs 1 crore a quarter. Your export was right about the rows and wrong about the units: it counts old Freshdesk refunds 100 times too large and counts some tickets twice. The attached workbook totals reconcile to the rupee.

**Why the numbers disagreed**
| Step | Rs |
|---|---|

The helpdesk's "about Rs 11 lakh a quarter" is in the same range as the clean quarters (Rs 12.1-16.3 lakh); we could not reproduce its exact definition.

**What happened to refunds.** Monthly refunds rose from Rs 1.4 lakh (Jan 2025) to a peak of Rs 5.8 lakh (Nov 2025) and have since held at Rs 3.7-4.5 lakh. Over the same period monthly tickets tripled (302 to 948 at peak). The share of tickets ending in a refund barely moved (17-23% every month) and the average refund stayed near Rs 2,900. So the data fits "more tickets, same refund rate", not "agents refunding more freely". Pulse 2 launched in July 2025 and accounts for Rs 5.4 lakh of the Rs 9.0 lakh rise from Q2 to Q4 2025; other products grew with their ticket volume. This is association, not cause: the data cannot say why tickets grew or test the Q4 change in frontline approach.

**Reasons.** The agents' reason code is unreliable: 43% of refund value (Rs 29 lakh) is coded "Goodwill/Other", the first item in the dropdown. Reading the ticket text, most of it is ordinary: refund delays after returns, payment problems, cancellations, product faults. On a 100-ticket check our reading matched in 96 to 97, but an AI assistant assigned those labels, so treat this as an estimate until a person has re-checked them. The workbook shows both views.

**Agents.** The workbook compares each agent with their own team, per refund ticket. The Returns Desk handles most refunds by design, so raw rupees mostly show who sits there. We make no finding about any individual.

**Where money leaks: refund plus replacement.** Policy says a customer must never get both. We found **368 tickets** where they did (166 flagged by agents, 202 only visible in closing notes). In the last four quarters that is about **65-75 a quarter**, costing about **Rs 1.2-1.4 lakh a quarter** in replacement units. They cluster in Chat Frontline, Logistics and Tier 2 (Returns Desk 4.5%, Billing 1%), pointing to process and tagging, not individuals.

**Proposed goal (our proposal, not a finding).** Cut refund+replacement tickets from about **65 to 10 or fewer a quarter**. Saving: about **Rs 1.0 lakh a quarter, Rs 4 lakh a year** (replacement units only, no refurbishment recovery). It is small next to Rs 13 lakh a quarter of total refunds; it is a control fix, not the cause of the rise.

**Confidence and limits.** High: reconciliation and monthly totals. Medium: reason-from-text (keyword rules, labels AI-made) and refund+replacement counts (about 50 of 368 rest on notes saying only "replacement dispatched"). Unknown: why volume grew; the helpdesk's Rs 11 lakh definition; dead-on-arrival vs warranty. 115 refunds on still-open tickets are included.
